Moderation keeps content bearable without you having to read along around the
clock. It is included in every plan.

## Reporting

Any member can report a comment or post. The available reasons are **spam**,
**harassment**, **off topic** and **other**, plus an optional note (up to 2,000
characters).

A report goes through four states: *open* → *under review* → *resolved* or
*rejected*. Open reports collect in the dashboard.

## Hiding and restoring

Hiding is **reversible**: the content disappears from the public view, but it
does not disappear from the world. You can make it visible again at any time.

When a comment is hidden, the **replies underneath** are hidden with it —
otherwise responses would stand there with nothing to refer to and the thread
would be unreadable.

## Automatic hiding

Optionally you can set a threshold: from *N* open reports onwards, content
disappears from the public view automatically. Two things matter here:

- The reports stay **open** — the automation decides nothing, it only buys
  time.
- A human closes the case afterwards: restore, or leave it hidden for good.
- The threshold can be switched off (default: off).

## AI as a second opinion

::note
**From the Pro plan**, and only if the AI function is set up on the instance.
::

At the press of a button, a language model gives an assessment of reported
content. That assessment is **advisory**: it hides nothing, deletes nothing and
closes no report. The decision is always made by a human. If the function isn't
available, the button is simply absent.

## Moderating in pairs

When several people moderate, the presence display shows who is currently
reviewing which case. That prevents the classic double work of two people
handling the same comment at the same time.

## Who may moderate

The **moderator** role and above. A moderator deliberately sees only the
moderation views — member management, design and billing stay closed to them
(see [Members & roles](/anleitung/mitglieder-und-rollen)).

## What moderation does not replace

Write down your **rules** as a page of your own (see
[Pages](/anleitung/produkte/seiten)). A short, visible rules page saves more
moderation work than any tool — and makes decisions comprehensible when you do
have to make them.
